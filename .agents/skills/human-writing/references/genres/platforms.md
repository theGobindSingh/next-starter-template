# Platforms

House rules change. Treat this as a starting point and tell the user to check the current rules before posting. Every platform below has an AI policy that matters; the skill keeps disclosures rather than hiding AI use.

## r/nosleep

- **Everything is true, even when it isn't.** Commenters stay in character and treat stories as real; the author doesn't break frame in the post.
- **Believability rule (loosened in the 2020s):** the narrator may die, be a ghost, an AI, a monster, in another dimension, incarcerated, or incapacitated **only if** there is a plausible explanation for how the post reached present-day r/nosleep. A change of narrator needs an explanation (the killer found the draft; a sibling posts it).
- **Banned:** revealing that the reader's world is a dream or simulation; stories where horror is not the point (horrible acts alone don't make horror; some topics may appear but not be the focus). Check the current rules for restricted topics.
- **Horror required.** Tone can be quiet or loud, but the piece must aim to frighten or unsettle.
- **Series:** each part labelled, posted at least 24 hours apart (the sub limits posting to about once per 24 hours).
- **AI-generated stories:** the community and moderators reject them; don't help pass off an AI-written story there. Help the user write their own, or edit their draft.
- Length: no hard rule; 1,500 to 5,000 words is common for single posts.

## Creepypasta sites (Creepypasta Wiki, creepypasta.com)

- Quality standards: correct spelling, grammar, and formatting; a real story, not a list of scary things.
- Clichés that get stories deleted or mocked: characters with red or bleeding eyes, haunted video game .exe files, spin-offs of famous pastas (Jeff the Killer, Slenderman, BEN Drowned), mentioning creepypastas inside a creepypasta, technically impossible events presented as real, "and then I saw it... it was me".
- Found-document and "lost episode" formats are traditional but crowded; a fresh angle is needed.

## Wattpad

- Serialized chapters; readers expect regular updates and chapter hooks. Chapters of 1,500 to 4,000 words are typical.
- Content ratings (mature content flagged); strong YA and romance audience.
- Monetization programs require attesting the work is created by you; AI-drafted work without disclosure risks removal.

## AO3 (Archive of Our Own)

- Tag system: Rating (General, Teen and Up, Mature, Explicit, Not Rated), Archive Warnings (Graphic Violence, Major Character Death, Rape/Non-Con, Underage, No Archive Warnings Apply, Creator Chose Not To Use Archive Warnings), relationships, characters, freeform tags.
- Author's notes at the top and bottom are normal here (unlike nosleep).
- AI-generated works are allowed by the Organization for Transformative Works; many readers filter them, and the community tag "Created Using Generative AI" exists. Keep the tag if AI generated the text.
- Fanfiction conventions: canon-compliant vs AU, tags are promises, respect characterization readers know.

## Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP)

- You must disclose AI-generated text, images, and translations when publishing or republishing; substantial human editing does not reclassify AI-generated text as AI-assisted. AI-assisted work (you wrote it, AI helped edit or brainstorm) needs no disclosure.
- Genre-standard lengths matter for reader expectations (see below). Front matter, a clean table of contents for ebooks, and consistent chapter formatting.
- KDP limits new title submissions per day and removes low-quality or undisclosed AI content.

## Magazine and contest submissions

- **AI policy:** most short-fiction markets now prohibit AI-written submissions (Clarkesworld closed submissions in 2023 after a flood of them). Check guidelines; many require you to affirm the work is yours.
- **Standard manuscript format** (the "Shunn" format most markets expect unless their guidelines say otherwise): 12-point Courier or Times New Roman, double-spaced, 1-inch margins; contact information top left; approximate word count top right (rounded); title and byline centered about a third to halfway down page one; header with surname, short title, and page number on later pages; indented paragraphs, no extra space between them; `#` centered for scene breaks; "END" at the close.
- Cover letter: short, plain, word count, title, one or two relevant credits if any. No plot summary unless asked.
- Simultaneous and multiple submissions: follow each market's stated policy.

## Word-count norms

| Form | Length |
|---|---|
| Drabble | 100 words |
| Microfiction | under ~300 |
| Flash fiction | under 1,000 (some markets 500 or 1,500) |
| Short story | 1,000–7,500 (SFWA/Nebula ceiling 7,500) |
| Novelette | 7,500–17,500 |
| Novella | 17,500–40,000 |
| Novel | 40,000+; adult genre novels commonly 70,000–100,000; epic fantasy often longer; category romance lines set their own lengths |
| nosleep post | commonly 1,500–5,000 per part |
| Wattpad chapter | commonly 1,500–4,000 |
