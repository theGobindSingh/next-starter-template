# Fantasy and science fiction

Speculative fiction promises a world that works differently and a story that could only happen there. The setting is not wallpaper; it creates the conflict.

## Worldbuilding without info-dumps

- **Iceberg rule:** know more than you show. Reveal the world through what characters use, fear, want, and take for granted.
- **No "As You Know, Bob"** exposition and no opening lore paragraph ("For a thousand years, the realm of Eldoria..."). Start with a character in trouble; let the reader infer.
- **Specific over grand.** One believable detail (the smell of the ration printer, a tax on dreams, how people swear) sells a world better than "ancient", "vast", "shimmering", "arcane".
- **Consequences.** If a technology or magic exists, ask what it changed: economics, crime, religion, manners, slang.
- **Consistent terms.** Invented words should be few, pronounceable, and used consistently. Don't capitalize every noun into a Proper Concept.

## Magic and technology

Sanderson's laws, widely used as guidelines:
1. An author's ability to solve conflict with magic is proportional to how well the reader understands that magic. (Soft, mysterious magic can create problems; hard, rule-based magic can solve them.)
2. Limitations are more interesting than powers. Costs and weaknesses make stories.
3. Expand what you already have before adding something new.

Hard SF adds: get the science right or make the deviation deliberate; readers will check.

## Subgenres

Epic/high fantasy · sword and sorcery · grimdark (moral ambiguity, violence, no clean heroes) · cozy fantasy (low stakes, community, comfort; conflict still needed) · urban fantasy · portal fantasy · fairy-tale retelling · romantasy (see `romance.md`) · hard SF · space opera · cyberpunk and post-cyberpunk · dystopian · solarpunk · first contact · time travel · military SF · climate fiction · New Weird.

## Genre-specific AI tells

- **Stock names and places:** Elara, Kael, Lyra, Aldric, Thorne, Seraphina, Nyx; Eldoria, Aetheria, the Shadowlands, the Whispering Woods; Dr. Aris Thorne, Dr. Elena Voss, Commander Voss (Gemini and GPT each have a favorite; see `../vocabulary.md`). Build names from the world's languages and cultures.
- **Mystic vocabulary kit:** ancient, arcane, ethereal, shimmering, runes glowing, crackling energy, a hum of power, the fabric of reality, the veil between worlds, ozone.
- **The prophecy and the chosen one** with no twist.
- **The wise mentor who explains everything** in one conversation.
- **Science by adjective:** "quantum resonance field", "a cascade failure in the neural lattice" with no understanding behind it.
- **Villain who wants to destroy the world** without reason; a Dark Lord.
- **Hopeful ending by default** even in grimdark or dystopia ("the rebellion's spark was lit").
- **Generic medieval Europe** with no economy, no food supply, and no ordinary people.
- **Opening with the lore dump** or a cosmic prologue.

## Checklist

- [ ] The world creates the central problem.
- [ ] Opening starts with a person and a problem, not lore.
- [ ] Magic/tech has costs and limits; the climax doesn't rely on unexplained power.
- [ ] Names built from the setting's own logic.
- [ ] At least three consequences of the speculative element shown in daily life.
- [ ] Tone and ending match the subgenre.
- [ ] `humanize_scan.py story.md --genre fantasy` (or `scifi`).
