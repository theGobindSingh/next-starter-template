# Romance

**Promise** (the genre's standard definition, as used by Romance Writers of America): a central love story and an emotionally satisfying, optimistic ending, happily ever after (HEA) or happy for now (HFN). A love story that ends in separation is literary fiction or a tragedy, not romance; tell the user if their request conflicts.

**Core value** (Story Grid): love versus hate/indifference. **Core event:** proof of love.

## Obligatory moments (Story Grid love genre)

- **Lovers meet** (meet-cute or meet-ugly): chemistry on the page, not summarized.
- **First kiss or first intimate connection**: they acknowledge the attraction.
- **Confession of love.**
- **Lovers break up** (the dark moment / black moment): something real forces them apart.
- **Proof of love**: one lover acts at real cost, often a sacrifice made without expecting to win the other back. This is the climax.
- **Lovers reunite** and commit.

These don't have fixed positions, but a romance missing one feels incomplete.

## Conventions

- A triangle or rival (a person, or a value like career, duty, a principle).
- Helpers and harmers around the couple.
- The lovers approach love and life differently; the relationship integrates them.
- An external need that forces them together.
- Opposing forces keeping them apart.
- Secrets: kept from each other, from society, from themselves (the flaw that blocks intimacy).
- Rituals of courtship.
- Moral weight: each must become a better version of themselves to deserve the love.

## Heat levels

Heat is a preference label, not a quality label, and scales vary by publisher. A practical 0–5 scale:

| Level | Name | On the page |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Sweet / clean | affection, maybe a kiss; no sexual content |
| 1 | Closed door / fade to black | kissing and tension; intimacy implied off-page |
| 2 | Warm | one or two scenes with some sensual detail, not explicit |
| 3 | Open door / steamy | explicit scenes on the page, emotionally central |
| 4–5 | Spicy / explicit | frequent, detailed explicit scenes |

Default is closed door. Keep heat consistent across chapters (heat drift from sweet to explicit with no transition reads like two books). Explicit content only for adult characters and always within the host platform's content rules; if the platform or context disallows it, write the scene closed door and say so.

## Common tropes

Enemies to lovers, friends to lovers, second chance, fake dating, forced proximity, grumpy/sunshine, forbidden love, marriage of convenience, slow burn, workplace, small town, one bed, secret identity, found family (often in romantasy). A trope is a promise; deliver its specific payoff (fake dating must get real; enemies must have been real enemies).

Subgenres: contemporary, historical (Regency, etc.), romantic suspense, paranormal, **romantasy** (romance + fantasy; both promises count, but the love story is usually the spine), rom-com, sports, LGBTQ+, inspirational (always closed door), dark romance, category romance (Harlequin-style lines with strict word counts and heat per line).

## Romance-specific AI tells

- **Conflict evaporates by conversation.** "They talked it through and understood each other." Real black moments cost something; misunderstandings rooted in character, not contrivance.
- **Therapy-speak dialogue.** "I hear you." "Your feelings are valid." "I need to set a boundary." "Thank you for sharing that with me." Perfect emotional literacy removes tension. Some characters talk like this; most do not, especially mid-fight.
- **Instant perfect communication** and instant trust; no misreadings, no pride, no deflection.
- **Identical banter**: both leads are witty in the same voice. Give them different speech rhythms and humor.
- **Stock physical beats:** electricity at a touch, breath hitching, heart skipping a beat, butterflies, "a smile tugging at the corner of his lips", "eyes darkened with desire", jaw ticking, a growl.
- **The generic love interest**: tall, dark hair, piercing (blue/grey/green) eyes, a scar with a story, a smirk, "possessive" as personality, named Kael, Silas, or Thorne.
- **Summarized chemistry.** "Over the next few weeks, they grew closer." The falling is the story; dramatize it.
- **Proof of love without cost**, or a grand gesture that is only a speech.
- **Endless epilogue** of the couple's perfect future, a baby, a wedding, and a restatement of what love taught them.

## Checklist

- [ ] Central love story is the spine; HEA or HFN planned.
- [ ] All six obligatory moments present (or knowingly adapted).
- [ ] Leads differ in voice, values, and wants; the conflict comes from who they are.
- [ ] Black moment has a real cause; proof of love costs something.
- [ ] Heat level stated and consistent.
- [ ] Trope payoff delivered.
